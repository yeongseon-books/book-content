Experiment Design: One Scene, 8 Styles

We generate the same scene in 8 styles. The shared scene description:

A rainy evening, a cozy independent bookshop with warm lighting turned on. Wet cobblestones reflecting the light.

Only the style keyword changes. Everything else stays identical.

1. Photorealistic

...photorealistic photography style, 35mm film grain

Photorealistic style: natural light behavior, water reflection on stone, material textures rendered faithfully.

Characteristics: Looks like a real photograph. Textures, reflections, and light falloff behave as a camera would capture them.

Use cases: Product photography, travel blogs, food photography, social media posts where realism sells.

Keywords: photorealistic, photography, 35mm film, DSLR, Canon EOS, shallow depth of field

2. Watercolor

...watercolor painting style, soft edges and color bleeding

Watercolor style: soft boundaries, colors bleeding into each other, a warm and immersive quality.

Characteristics: Edges are soft and diffused. Colors bleed and overlap. The overall feel is warm and dreamy.

Use cases: Invitation cards, postcards, emotional blog thumbnails, lifestyle social content.

Keywords: watercolor, watercolour painting, soft washes, color bleeding, wet-on-wet

3. Oil Painting

...oil painting style, thick impasto brushstrokes, rich saturated colors

Oil painting style: thick visible brushstrokes, deep saturated colors. Compared to watercolor, it feels heavier and more textured.

Characteristics: Visible brushwork with impasto texture. Colors are deep and saturated. The image carries weight and physical presence that watercolor lacks.

Watercolor vs Oil Painting comparison:

Edges
- Watercolor: Soft and bleeding
- Oil Painting: Defined but textured

Colors
- Watercolor: Transparent and light
- Oil Painting: Deep and dense

Mood
- Watercolor: Light and dreamy
- Oil Painting: Heavy and dramatic

Best for
- Watercolor: Flowers, landscapes, lighthearted scenes
- Oil Painting: Portraits, cityscapes, dramatic scenes

Keywords: oil painting, impasto, thick brushstrokes, rich colors, gallery painting

4. Pixel Art

...pixel art style, 16-bit retro game aesthetic, limited color palette

Pixel art style: dot-based graphics with a retro game feel. Simple but distinctive.

Characteristics: Retro game aesthetic. A limited color palette forces deliberate choices, creating a nostalgic and playful mood.

Use cases: Gaming content, tech blogs, developer community posts, distinctive thumbnails.

Keywords: pixel art, 8-bit, 16-bit, retro game, sprite, limited palette

5. Anime/Manga

...anime illustration style, Studio Ghibli inspired, soft pastel colors

Anime style: soft pastel tones with detailed background rendering. Warm and nostalgic atmosphere.

Characteristics: Detailed backgrounds with soft color palettes. Creates a warm, inviting mood that feels both familiar and fantastical.

Use cases: YouTube thumbnails, storytelling content, character-driven posts.

Keywords: anime style, manga illustration, Studio Ghibli, Makoto Shinkai, cel shading, pastel colors

6. 3D Render

...3D render style, Pixar-like aesthetic, smooth surfaces, volumetric lighting

3D render style: smooth surfaces, soft volumetric lighting. Evokes Pixar/Disney animation.

Characteristics: Clean smooth surfaces, soft lighting with volumetric quality. Feels polished and modern — like a frame from an animated film.

Use cases: App/UI mockups, cute branding, children's content, product introductions.

Keywords: 3D render, Pixar style, Blender, Cinema 4D, smooth, volumetric lighting, octane render

7. Flat Vector

...flat vector illustration style, clean geometric shapes, limited flat color palette, no gradients

Flat vector style: clean geometric shapes and flat colors. Optimized for digital content.

Characteristics: Clean and simple. No gradients — flat color planes and geometric shapes. Scales perfectly and looks professional at any size.

Use cases: Website illustrations, app icons, infographics, presentation diagrams.

Keywords: flat vector, flat design, geometric, minimal illustration, no gradients, SVG style

8. Pencil Sketch

...pencil sketch style, detailed line work, cross-hatching for shadows, black and white

Pencil sketch style: line detail and cross-hatching convey depth without color. Traditional and intellectual.

Characteristics: Black and white line drawing. Cross-hatching creates tonal range without color. Feels traditional, academic, and intentional.

Use cases: Architecture visualization, concept art, academic content, journal/notebook aesthetics.

Keywords: pencil sketch, pencil drawing, line art, cross-hatching, graphite, black and white

Style Selection Guide

A quick reference for choosing the right style based on your goal.

Product shots, food photos
- Recommended Style: Photorealistic
- Why: Realism builds trust

Emotional blogs, invitations
- Recommended Style: Watercolor
- Why: Soft and warm

Portfolio, artistic content
- Recommended Style: Oil Painting
- Why: Weight and depth

Gaming/tech blogs
- Recommended Style: Pixel Art
- Why: Distinctive and playful

Character-driven content
- Recommended Style: Anime
- Why: Approachable and expressive

App/web design mockups
- Recommended Style: 3D Render / Flat Vector
- Why: Clean and modern

Academic, conceptual
- Recommended Style: Pencil Sketch
- Why: Professional and intellectual

Combining Styles

You're not limited to a single style. Combinations often produce distinctive results.

Effective combinations:

Photo + Cinema
- Prompt Keywords: cinematic photography, anamorphic lens
- Result: Dramatic movie-frame feel

Watercolor + Digital
- Prompt Keywords: digital watercolor, clean edges
- Result: Watercolor mood with sharper definition

Pixel + Anime
- Prompt Keywords: pixel art anime style
- Result: Charming retro-meets-Japan aesthetic

3D + Isometric
- Prompt Keywords: 3D isometric render
- Result: Diagram/map-like clarity

Key Takeaway: Style as the Strongest Lever

  • Style is the single most powerful element for setting an image's overall mood
  • Each style has a distinct personality and use case — knowing them prevents trial-and-error
  • Combining styles gives you unique results that stand out from default outputs

Answering the Opening Questions

When is photorealistic more appropriate than illustration?

Photorealistic works when you need the viewer to trust what they see — product photos, food, travel. Illustration styles work when you need to convey mood, concept, or personality over literal accuracy.

How do watercolor and oil painting actually differ?

Watercolor has soft bleeding edges, transparent colors, and feels light. Oil painting has thick visible brushwork, saturated colors, and feels heavy. Use watercolor for emotional/lighthearted scenes, oil painting for dramatic/weighty scenes.

Which style for blogs, social media, and presentations?

Blogs: photorealistic (product/review) or flat vector (technical explanation). Social media: anime (character-driven) or watercolor (emotional). Presentations: flat vector (diagrams) or 3D render (mockups).
